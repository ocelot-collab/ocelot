## B. Independent physics review

### Magnet frames, optics about an orbit and mode matching

* **Offsets in a linear magnet.** Translating a straight quadrupole gives the affine offset $(I-R)d$. It steers an initially centred particle, while the derivative remains $R$. A thin normal sextupole at a horizontal orbit offset $x_0$ instead gives an additional horizontal kick gradient $-K_2L x_0$. For a linear quadrupole the particle orbit changes but the transfer matrix does not. In a sextupole the orbit offset changes the matrix itself. The corresponding simple lattices, their controls and the observed differences appear in section E.
* **Survey and mechanical roll.** A design roll of a sector bend rotates its surveyed bending plane. A straight quadrupole retains its survey axis. An installed sector rotated relative to that plane needs distinct entrance and exit transforms, intersections with its faces, and the corresponding path/time change. In a 0.6 m sector with angle 0.13 rad and 2.5 mrad installation roll, fresh MAD-X 5.09.03 calculations and four Ocelot tracking methods agreed within $1.8\times10^{-11}$ in transverse exit coordinates (positions in m, momenta in units of reference momentum). The oblique finite-energy face Jacobian preserved the appropriate two-form to $1.1\times10^{-16}$. This validates the ideal sector benchmark. Arbitrary fringe-field measurements were outside it.
* **Local Jacobians and composed maps.** For $F(z)=B+Rz+T[z,z]$, the derivative at $u$ is $R+T[u,\cdot]+T[\cdot,u]$. A downstream nonlinear map acting on the upstream offset changes both the linear term and the constant. An independent scalar polynomial composition reproduced the implemented translated quadratic coefficients to $1.1\times10^{-16}$. The result is a Taylor polynomial through order two. Truncating after composition limits its use at large amplitudes. At a real orbit the active tracker is differentiated instead. Analytical magnetic/RF derivatives and finite differences of other active maps have different precision.
* **Matched normal modes.** The implementation solves the fixed-energy transverse closed orbit, diagonalizes the symplectic four-dimensional one-turn Jacobian and normalizes each stable eigenvector by its symplectic action. Mode emittances weight the two covariances. For a tilted FODO, MAD-X gave tunes 0.089729950 and 0.043050550. Ocelot differed by at most $1.4\times10^{-16}$ in tune and $3.2\times10^{-14}$ m in projected beta with unequal mode emittances. Conjugating two independent oscillators by a known symplectic transform gave covariance closure below $1.2\times10^{-19}$ for phase separations of 0.4, $10^{-7}$, $10^{-11}$ and 0 rad. An exactly equal-tune subspace has no unique distribution of unequal emittances without specifying a basis. Close to degeneracy, mode labels can change as the machine changes. A mode's projected phase is undefined at zero projection. A six-dimensional synchrobetatron normal-form solver would be different functionality.
* **Acceleration and historical dispersion.** Projected beam sizes follow covariance transport. A periodic cell now matches the ellipse using the transverse block determinant and the dispersion using the composed design-dispersion transport. At 2 MeV entrance, 2 MV gain and entrance $D_x=0.2$ m, the standing-wave example gave 0.17734 m for the historical design convention and 0.25729 m per unit local normalized energy deviation using the tracked orbit. The corresponding travelling-wave values were 0.18874 and 0.27034 m. These APIs deliberately retain different definitions through RF. For comparison to a small physical energy offset use `twiss(..., reference_orbit=Particle())` and specify whether the normalization is local or at the entrance.

### RF, tracking, synchrotron radiation and analytical transport

* **Standing- and travelling-wave cavities.** A finite RF field still focuses at a zero crossing. For the standing-wave relativistic transverse model the zero-gain limit is a Hill oscillator with strength $(V/(\sqrt{8}EL))^2$. A uniform-gradient ODE including entrance/exit lenses reproduced the corrected transverse matrix within $2.2\times10^{-14}$ across 11 phases including both zero crossings. At 50 MeV and 30 MV, this gives an observable downstream change of 0.190 mm for a 1 mm offset. For travelling-wave transport, independent quadrature of $p_i\int ds/p(s)$ agreed with $R_{12}$ to $3.4\times10^{-16}$ m in the tested cases at 0.7, 2 and 50 MeV. RF energy uses the time coordinate $\tau=c(t-t_{ref})$ and phase $\phi-k\tau$. Longitudinal second-order coefficients were checked against derivatives of the integrated inverse speed. RF edge, uniform-gradient and higher-order approximations still apply. An accelerating cell whose ellipse shape repeats does not describe a fixed-energy closed ring. [Rosenzweig and Serafini](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.49.1599)
* **RK, MAD and XYQuadrupole.** The RK state carries transverse mechanical momenta normalized to reference momentum. Slopes are calculated with longitudinal momentum and the flight time uses each particle's speed. Two off-energy electrons on exact helices in a constant axial field at 2 MeV and 0.8 m agreed to $3.4\times10^{-11}$ m in position and $3.1\times10^{-12}$ m in arrival time at 200 RK4 steps. Global Cartesian and transported normal output planes answer different questions. The MAD tensor tests integrate the cubic-Hamiltonian variational equations independently, including zero focusing, the mixed-frequency resonance and negative length. A separate adaptive Lorentz integration with an exit-plane event tested the weak-curvature XYQuadrupole dispersion. Relative errors fell from $1.64\times10^{-5}$ to $1.94\times10^{-7}$ to $2.67\times10^{-9}$ as the entrance x offset fell from 10 to 1 to 0.1 mm. The analytical model assumes small reference slopes and weak curvature and has an incoming-reference convention distinct from a misaligned ordinary quadrupole.
* **Radiation and quantum diffusion.** The on-axis fundamental from $\lambda_u(1+K^2/2)/(2\gamma^2)$ was 14.9879 eV, at the peak of a scan with 0.060 eV spacing across ±10% of the predicted energy. Coherent fields require each particle's entrance time phase and the relative optical distance from its transverse source position to each screen pixel. Two identical simultaneous sources separated by 0.4 mm yielded a first minimum near 0.090 mrad against 0.09244 mrad from path difference, at 0.005 mrad resolution. A common random energy kick shifts the entire bunch without generating the predicted spread. The correction draws independent kicks, and a planar undulator rotated into the other transverse plane has the same loss and diffusion. Absolute predictions for arbitrary custom fields, helical diffusion and radiation phase within accelerating sections have separate modelling limits. [Tomin et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-023-28813-8)

### Collective effects, diagnostics and beam interchange

* **CSR.** The corrected kernel integrates along positive source-to-observer intervals. On a circle $K=-\sin(\phi/2)/\rho$ integrates to $-4\sin^2(\phi/4)$. The particle arrival-time coordinate is converted to spatial position by $z=-\beta\tau$, and the force is read at the same mesh origin used to deposit charge. For a 1 GeV Gaussian bunch, radius 4 m and 100 µm rms length, independent quadrature of the differentiated line density against the steady-state kernel at 80 bins gave the signed head-to-tail wake to within 0.002 in units of $Q/(4\pi\epsilon_0\rho^{2/3}\sigma_z^{4/3})$ at five positions. The rms coefficient at 320 bins was 0.24544 against the theoretical value about 0.2459. Tests also cover total charge, unequal weights, overlapping bins, the horizontal-to-vertical bend frame and physical path length. This is a free-space 1D model. SaferCSR's transverse-validity warning is approximate in a skew, flat, fully 3D beam. [Khan and Raubenheimer](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevAccelBeams.24.080701)
* **IBS.** The linac model adds longitudinal energy variance for a round, nearly Gaussian beam. Huang supplies the cold limit. The Nagaitsev option supplies a finite-temperature fit. An independent comparison with the Carlson-integral expression over 500 temperature ratios from $10^{-15}$ to 0.999999 gave a maximum relative fit discrepancy of 12.47%. The cold-bunch simulation at 250 pC produced 253.25 eV rms against a rate-integral prediction of 254.38 eV. The heating model omits three-plane equilibration and cooling. [Huang](https://www-ssrl.slac.stanford.edu/lcls/technotes/LCLS-TN-02-8.pdf) · [Nagaitsev](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevSTAB.8.064403)
* **PMD and beam analysis.** The reference is set by mean total energy; divide transverse momenta by the momentum corresponding to that energy. For a broad low-energy test distribution the previous import shifted transverse momenta by 9.47% and produced a non-real longitudinal momentum for a slow particle. The corrected forward-electron round trip reproduced all three Cartesian momenta to relative $3\times10^{-14}$. Reference changes preserve individual absolute energies and transverse momenta. List/array conversion retains charge and reference metadata. The PMD interchange still uses the spatial-bunch approximation $\tau=-z$; an independent external arrival time is not imported.
* **Passive streaker.** Integrating the single-particle energy change across independent centred Gaussian transverse coordinates gives a quadrupole correction to the mean proportional to $\sigma_x^2-\sigma_y^2$, and dipole and quadrupole contributions to the variance. A deterministic Gauss–Hermite rule applied to individual particle energies reproduces both moments to relative $2\times10^{-15}$. A round slice can have zero quadrupole mean shift and nonzero broadening. The spillover term uses the outgoing chirp. The single-plate characteristic scales for monopole and quadrupole wakes have different coefficients, 8/9 and 1/2. This scalar slice model assumes independent transverse coordinates and Gaussian fourth moments. [Dijkstal, Qin and Tomin](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevAccelBeams.27.050702) · [Qin, Dohlus and Zagorodnov](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevAccelBeams.26.064402)

### Matching, response, and photon analysis

* A phase target measures propagated phase from **entrance of start** to **exit of end**, in radians modulo the selected periodicity. For an uncoupled map its independent check is $\operatorname{atan2}(R_{12},\beta_iR_{11}-\alpha_i R_{12})$. A tune from a periodic trace is a different quantity on an open line. A 7 m quadrupole with unit matched beta and unit focusing now yields 7 rad when sampled densely. An isolated endpoint matrix cannot distinguish phase advances differing by whole turns.
* A thin kick followed by a drift of length $L$ gives an orbit response $L$ m/rad. In a ring the linear launch solves $(I-M_{4\times4})X=B$. The direct dispersion response propagates the directional derivative of the Jacobian; a finite simulated entrance-energy difference has truncation error. An RF station scan instead changes the cavity voltage, keeping magnets fixed and accounting for the changed arrival clock. In the new 0.1 rad sector example the last BPM gave $D_x=0.219542$ m against 0.219650 m from the infinitesimal ultrarelativistic formula. A 100 kV scan on a 200 MeV beam has finite amplitude. Direct linear response assumes the stated optics model; coupling, acceleration or nonlinear feed-down can invalidate the simple two-plane beta/phase formula.
* Transverse resampling of a complex field should preserve slice power over an unchanged aperture. The previous spline code dropped the last row and column of the input norm, even for a flat field. A 7×9 uniform field lost 15/63 of its power. For uniform input a central aperture half as wide and half as high now transmits 1/4 of the power. Other tests check phase of complex polynomials, zero fill and dark slices. The resampling convention treats each source pixel’s intensity as constant over its area for the retained-power estimate. If a coarse grid misses every illuminated pixel despite positive retained power, the routine raises an error.
* **Undulator wavefronts and diffraction.** A direct sum over all 120 source pixels on a 12×10 grid gave a reference independent of the FFT convolution for a finite-emittance beam. Two 2167 eV slices had different launch angles. Relative peak field differences were below 2.1 × 10⁻¹⁵ for source intensity and amplitude convolution and below 5.7 × 10⁻¹⁶ for their angular counterparts. Vacuum propagation of DC and tilted modes on even and odd grids checked the use of shifted discrete Fourier frequencies for transverse wave numbers.
* Genesis matrices store positive alpha off-diagonal; a physical particle covariance contains negative alpha. Independent covariance and determinant checks on four ellipses, including opposite alpha signs, verified both returned maps to relative $1.6\times10^{-15}$. A padded monochromatic time signal retains its sample timestamps and lands at the predicted photon energy. For geometric optics a tilted plane must project the hit into an orthonormal surface frame before binning the hit. The detector has a rectangular pixel grid and an elliptical active aperture. A different candidate that misses its aperture must leave the accepted hit intact. The legacy elliptic-mirror implementation assumes a special global y–z geometry; a general posed curved surface requires a separate implementation and validation.

**Performance.** On this host, seven alternating warm runs comparing `1c27bd4a` with `bad4983c` gave 1.115–1.24× CSR speedups at 10k and 100k particles and two binning settings. Relative L2 kick change attributable to that optimization was at most $3.1\times10^{-15}$. That comparison isolates the historical speed change. The later physics fixes legitimately change results. Full Cartesian RK bunch tracking holds the current state and path; the SR integrator retains sampled trajectories because the radiation integral needs them. No end-to-end speedup is claimed for this review patch.

**Compatibility.** The historical range raises the minimum Python version to 3.11, separates periodic initialization from transport, removes Floquet targets, changes seeded-error output from ID-keyed values to occurrence records, and renames/removes CSR options. The branch rejects superseded CSR keywords and names their replacements. Numerical results can change with these local corrections, especially for CSR, zero-crossing RF, coherent sources, and photon interpolation. Existing scripts distinguishing design and local dispersion can continue to use their chosen convention.

## D. Documentation and typing

* The tracked-orbit module's private `_OrbitMap` carries the orbit, reference energy, path coordinate and accumulated derivative. The public examples use `twiss` and `periodic_twiss`. The module docstrings already explained much of this at HEAD. Clarifications in `optics.py` and `beam/core.py` cover what actually repeats in an accelerating cell, sampling, projected covariance and the dispersion denominator.
* `magnet_alignment.FaceTransform` now explains why a particle hits a rotated physical end plane at a different point and time. The code documents the local/nominal frames. `transformations/linearization.py:23` already documented the active local Jacobian and its directional derivative, including the sextupole example. That interface was preserved.
* RF and RK documentation now distinguishes entrance reference energy, particle energy, normalized momenta, slopes, path length and timing. The local public [cavity page](../ocelot-collab.github.io/docs/docu/elements/cavity.md) gives finite zero-crossing expressions; the [CSR page](../ocelot-collab.github.io/docs/docu/physics-processes/csr.md) explains the spatial bunch coordinate. `ocelot/rad/genera.md` describes coherent phase, loss, diffusion and the within-segment approximation.
* Public or important numerical signatures gained plain annotations for energies, lengths, covariances or arrays in the RF, MAD, XYQuadrupole, alignment, response, IBS, beam and streaker paths. A local wheel build verified that the distribution includes the type marker. Whole-package static type checking was outside this audit.

**Model limits requiring judgment.** Exactly coincident transverse tunes need a mode-basis convention to assign unequal emittances. Fixed-energy closed-orbit modes omit longitudinal synchrobetatron motion. The two RF dispersion conventions differ at low energy. Mixed-element SR assumes straight laboratory placement and one reference energy per sampled radiating segment. Extreme skew or transverse extent weakens the 1D CSR validity estimate. The legacy RadiationField real-space labels span both box endpoints, and its longitudinal spectral axis uses an endpoint-based grid. The transverse DFT bin tests certify the angular scale only. A change to the shared longitudinal and real-space conventions needs a separate review of existing photon workflows.
